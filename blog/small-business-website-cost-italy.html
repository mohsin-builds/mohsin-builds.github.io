How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost in Italy?

The right website budget starts with a clear business goal, not a generic price list.

MOHSIN BUILDS23 OCTOBER 20268 MIN READ
01 / website-budget-guide.md23 OCT 2026

Two website quotes can look very different because they cover different work. A simple information site, a multilingual service site and an online store are not equivalent projects. Before you compare prices, compare what is actually included.

Quick answer: A useful quote defines the pages, design, content, functionality, testing, ownership and ongoing costs. Asking for a fixed number without that scope invites misunderstandings.

1. Define the business outcome

Choose a primary action: customers should call, request a quote, make a reservation or buy a product. The site's navigation, content and features should support that action.

Write down your typical customer, what they need to know and the next action you want them to take. This is more valuable than starting with color choices or animations.

2. Know the main cost drivers

ComponentWhat to clarify
Number of pagesHow many unique designs and page types are needed?
ContentWho writes the copy, supplies photos and translates text?
DesignTemplate adaptation or custom layout? Responsive and accessible?
FunctionsForms, bookings, multilingual support, payments and integrations?
Search readinessTitles, redirects, structured content and technical checks?
MaintenanceWho handles backups, updates, renewals and support?

3. Start with the smallest useful scope

A local service business may need just a few clear pages, a quote form and accurate contact details. A restaurant may need an accessible menu and reservation flow. E-commerce adds product management, payment compliance and fulfillment workflows. Choose for the problem you have today, while preserving room for growth.

4. Budget for ongoing costs

Ask for recurring costs in writing: domain renewal, hosting, premium software, transaction charges, maintenance and optional support. Verify who owns the domain, hosting account and finished files. Costs and tax treatment depend on the provider and the project; avoid relying on a quote that doesn't explain them.

5. Compare quotes fairly

  1. What exactly will be delivered, and how many revision rounds?
  2. Who supplies and owns the text, images, domain and code?
  3. Are mobile layouts, forms and accessibility included?
  4. Are third-party service fees and VAT included or separate?
  5. How will the website be tested before launch?
  6. What happens when the site needs updates later?

6. Prepare a one-page project brief

Business: name, location and offering

Main goal: bookings, calls, quote requests or sales

Essential pages: home, services, about, contact

Features: forms, reservations, payments, languages

Materials: existing domain, logo, photos, copy

Constraints: budget range, deadlines, ongoing maintenance

With that brief, you can compare providers on what your business actually needs rather than just a headline price.
